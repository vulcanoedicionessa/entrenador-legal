# Non-Clinical Notice · Climbing Coach

> PENDING LEGAL REVIEW — draft not yet approved.

Read this before you start. It is short and it tells you what the app can do for you and where its role ends.

## 1. What this app is and what it is not

This app helps you organise and record your climbing training. It proposes sessions, keeps count of your load and reminds you of what the book says about when to stop. Nothing more.

The app **does not diagnose** anything. **It does not replace a healthcare professional** or a clinical examination. Its messages are not a diagnostic guide: use them to name what you feel and to decide whether to keep training or make an appointment. When the app tells you "consult", the decision to go and the diagnosis belong to a professional, not to the app and not to you.

If you have an injury, an illness or any doubt about whether you can train, consult before you start.

## 2. Stop signals

These are the four stop signals used by the collection's training journal. If one of these four appears, the session is not modified: it is abandoned.

- An audible pop on loading, with swelling on the palmar side of the finger.
- Diffuse pain along the path of the flexor, with morning stiffness.
- A niggle still there more than two weeks after you lowered the load.
- Pain on the dorsal side of the middle joint of a finger if you are under 18.

In all four: stop loading and consult a healthcare professional.

## 3. When you stop deciding: the five referral criteria

This is the referral rule from the book *Climb Stronger* (Chapter 10). It is explicit and is not left to your judgement of the day. Consult a healthcare professional as soon as a **single one** of these five criteria is met, without waiting for them to accumulate.

1. An audible pop in a finger with immediate swelling.
2. Pain on the dorsal side of the middle joint of a finger in someone under 18.
3. A niggle that persists for more than 2 weeks despite your having reduced the load.
4. Loss of strength, joint locking or a sensation of instability.
5. Pain that forces you to modify your technique in order to climb.

None of them requires a prior diagnosis from you: they are observations you can make without clinical knowledge. A niggle that settles in under 24 h belongs to training; one that persists for more than 2 weeks despite your having lowered the load belongs to the clinic.

## 4. What the app does with these signals

The app reminds you of these signals and puts them in front of you when it matters:

- When you close each session you can tick the stop signals you noticed. If you tick sharp pain or a pop in a finger, the app closes that day's finger work and does not schedule finger loading for the following 48 hours.
- If a niggle you have recorded is still there after more than three days, the app suggests you consult and opens the niggles and injuries log so that you can write it down.
- The five referral criteria are shown in the app exactly as they appear in section 3, with none added or removed.

These messages are reminders the app generates from what you record. They detect nothing by themselves, they do not assess your condition and they do not replace the rule in section 3: if a criterion is met, consult even if the app has not warned you.

## 5. What you will not find in the app

There is no self-treatment protocol here and no phased return progression. For pulley injury, flexor tenosynovitis, the adolescent's growth-plate injury or any other injury, management happens after a professional diagnosis and with clinical follow-up. Your role ends at recognising the signal, stopping loading and making an appointment. The first thing that is adjusted is the load, and it is also the first thing that is done in the clinic.

The app also gives you no advice on diet or weight, and it does not store your weight or your body composition.

## 6. If you are under 18

You can use the app from the age of 16. If you are under 18, pay attention to criterion 2 in section 3: any pain on the dorsal side of the middle joint of a finger is grounds for consulting before anything is decided about the load.

## 7. In an emergency

In a medical emergency, go to the emergency services in your area. The app is not a healthcare channel and nobody supervises it in real time.
